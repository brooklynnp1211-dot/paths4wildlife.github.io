What is a wildlife corridor?

Creating pathways through fragmented habitats

Wildlife corridors are areas of land or habitat that connect separated populations or habitat patches. These connections can allow animals to move between areas that they need for feeding, breeding, migration, and shelter.

Habitat fragmentation can make it difficult for wildlife to move across landscapes. Corridors can help maintain connectivity and allow populations to remain connected.

Beyond ecology

Corridors affect people, too.

Wildlife corridors exist within landscapes that are also used by people for housing, transportation, agriculture, recreation, and other activities. Because of this, corridor planning can involve ecological, political, economic, and social considerations.
