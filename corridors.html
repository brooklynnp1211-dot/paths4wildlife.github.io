WHAT IS A WILDLIFE CORRIDOR?

Creating pathways through fragmented habitats

Wildlife corridors are areas of land or habitat that connect separated populations or habitat patches. These connections can allow animals to move between areas that they need for feeding, breeding, migration, and shelter.

Habitat fragmentation can make it difficult for wildlife to move across landscapes. Corridors can help maintain connectivity and allow populations to remain connected.

ECOLOGICAL BENEFITS

What do wildlife corridors do?

Corridors can provide several ecological functions. Click each topic to learn more.

🦌 Movement

Corridors can provide pathways that allow animals to move between different habitat patches. Movement may be important for finding food, water, mates, shelter, and seasonal habitats.

FROM CORRIDORS TO GENETICS

Does a corridor actually work?

A corridor may appear connected on a map, but researchers also need evidence that animals are actually using it. Genetic data can provide evidence of connectivity by helping researchers investigate gene flow between populations.

THE HUMAN CONNECTION

Corridors are more than ecological pathways.

Wildlife corridors often cross landscapes that are also used by people. Roads, farms, private property, and communities can all influence where corridors can be established and how they are managed.
