WELCOME

From DNA to the landscape

Wildlife corridors are designed to reconnect fragmented habitats and allow animals to move between populations. But how do we know if these corridors are actually functioning?

Genetic data can provide evidence of animal movement and gene flow across landscapes. By combining genetics with ecology, policy, and sociology, we can develop a broader understanding of wildlife corridor conservation.

WHY DOES THIS MATTER?

Connecting wildlife across fragmented landscapes

As roads, cities, agriculture, and other human activities change landscapes, wildlife populations can become increasingly isolated.

Wildlife corridors can help reconnect these populations by providing pathways for movement. Understanding whether those pathways actually function is an important part of conservation planning.

Genetic data gives researchers another way to investigate connectivity by examining patterns of gene flow between populations.
