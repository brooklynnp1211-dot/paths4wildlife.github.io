Protecting wildlife corridors requires more than connecting habitats. It also means making thoughtful, equitable decisions about land, resources, communities, and the future.

THE ROLE OF POLICY

Conservation Decisions Shape Our Landscapes

Wildlife corridors help reconnect fragmented habitats, allowing animals to move between areas, maintain genetic connectivity, and access important resources. However, the protection and management of these corridors often depend on environmental policies, land-use planning, funding, and cooperation between governments, conservation organizations, landowners, and local communities.

Conservation policies influence which landscapes are protected, how development is managed, and how the benefits and responsibilities of conservation are shared. Understanding these decisions is important for creating wildlife corridors that are ecologically effective and socially sustainable.

ENVIRONMENTAL JUSTICE

Conservation Should Be Fair for Wildlife and People

Environmental justice focuses on ensuring that environmental benefits, burdens, and decision-making opportunities are distributed fairly among communities.

🌿

Environmental Benefits

Wildlife corridors can provide ecological and social benefits that extend beyond wildlife.

  • Healthier and more connected ecosystems
  • Protection of biodiversity
  • Improved opportunities for recreation and outdoor activities
  • Potential economic opportunities through ecotourism
  • Environmental benefits for surrounding communities
⚖️

Responsibilities & Challenges

Corridor conservation can also create responsibilities and challenges that must be considered.

  • Who is responsible for corridor maintenance?
  • Who may experience restrictions on land use?
  • How are conservation costs distributed?
  • Are local communities included in decisions?
  • Could some communities experience greater impacts than others?

COMMUNITY INVOLVEMENT

Whose Voices Are Included?

Wildlife corridor planning can affect landowners, residents, farmers, businesses, and communities near protected areas. Conservation decisions should consider the experiences and concerns of the people who live and work in these landscapes.

Including community perspectives can help identify potential conflicts, develop practical solutions, and create conservation strategies that are more likely to succeed over time.

A BALANCED APPROACH

Connecting Conservation and Community

Successful wildlife corridor conservation considers ecological needs while recognizing the social and economic realities of surrounding communities.

People & Communities

THE GOAL

Conservation That Benefits Both Wildlife and People

Long-term conservation success depends on protecting ecological connections while building policies that are inclusive, sustainable, and fair.

LOOKING FORWARD

Conservation Is Both an Ecological and Social Process

Wildlife corridors are not simply lines connecting habitats. They are part of landscapes shared by wildlife and people. Conservation policies must consider scientific evidence, environmental protection, community needs, and fairness.

By integrating ecological research with environmental justice and community participation, wildlife corridor conservation can become more effective, inclusive, and sustainable.
